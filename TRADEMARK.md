## What the name means

*Geshtu* (also transliterated *Geštu* or *Geštug*) is Sumerian for
**"ear", "wisdom", and "memory"** — the same word for all three. In
Mesopotamian thought the ear, not the brain, was the organ of memory:
to listen well *was* to remember. The Sumerian deity **Geshtu-(e)** is
the god of intelligence; according to the Atrahasis epic, his blood
was mixed with clay to create humanity, so that humans would carry
memory inside them.

Five thousand years later, teams using LLMs still need somewhere to
keep what they have heard. The name is meant literally.

## Forks and derivatives

Forks are welcome and encouraged — please choose a different name. If
your fork stays close to upstream and you want to refer to its origin,
"based on Geshtu" is fine; using "Geshtu" as the brand of
the fork itself is not.

## Why this policy exists

This is standard practice for OSS projects of this kind — the Linux
Foundation, Mozilla, Sentry, GitLab, and HashiCorp all maintain similar
policies. It protects users from confusion (so that "Geshtu" on
GitHub means the same thing as "Geshtu" in a Slack message six
months from now) and protects the project's ability to remain neutral
and independent.
